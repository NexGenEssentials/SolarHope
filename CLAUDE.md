## 2. What it actually sells

Hybrid solar PV systems: solar panels + hybrid inverter + lithium (LiFePO4) battery,
installed alongside the client's existing REG grid connection with **automatic changeover**.
The grid stays connected. Nothing is off-grid unless the client has no grid at all.

**Three system set-ups (confirmed 22 Nov 2026), each available with manual or automatic
switching:**
1. **Backup for power outages:** the system only takes over when the grid goes off. It
   recharges from solar panels or from the grid, depending on the client's needs.
2. **Hybrid:** the sun runs things first, the battery stores the extra for the evening,
   and the grid tops up when needed.
3. **Off-grid first, grid as backup:** the client runs on solar and battery; the grid
   only comes in when the battery runs low or during long cloudy spells.

**Switching:** automatic is the default and the recommendation; automatic set-ups also
include a manual override. A manual-only set-up is possible on request. Copy may say the
system "switches over by itself" as the standard, but should not claim it is the only way.

Scope of a typical job: site assessment → design → supply → installation → testing and
commissioning → staff training → handover → warranty and remote monitoring.

Customer segments, in order of commercial importance:

| Segment | What drives the sale |
|---|---|
| Businesses & SMEs | Cutting a large monthly REG bill, and not losing trade during outages |
| Schools | Evening study, computer labs, boarding facilities; they pay the subsidised 214 tariff |
| Health facilities | Continuity — refrigeration, equipment, no tolerance for outages |
| Households | Cost and reliability |
| NGOs / institutions | Grant- or donor-funded installations, often multi-site |

**Home EV charging powered by solar (confirmed 22 Nov 2026):** SolarHope sets up home
charging for electric vehicles, powered by the client's solar system. **SolarHope supplies
the power only, not the charger:** it sizes and connects the solar system to run the
client's own EV charger. Never say or imply SolarHope sells chargers.
- Mainly **electric cars**, but the setup can power any EV charger the client has.
- Charging works **day and night**: from the panels by day, from the home battery at night.
- Pricing: **on request** (quoted per home); do not publish a price.
- Honesty rule, same as backup: a car battery (often 40–60 kWh) is larger than most
  home batteries, so night charging from the battery is a top-up, not a full charge.
  Never imply a full overnight charge on solar; how much depends on battery size and
  the household's other evening use.
- Belongs on the Homes page, not the Businesses page.

A fifth line worth building out: **repairs and upgrades** of existing failed or badly
installed systems. The company has done several of these and it is an underserved market.

## 4. Numbers the copy must get right

**Sizing rule used across all SolarHope material:**

```
daily generation (kWh) = array kWp × 4.0 peak sun hours × 0.80 system efficiency
```

One 445 Wp panel ≈ **1.42 kWh/day**. One 450 Wp panel ≈ **1.44 kWh/day**.
Usable battery capacity = **90% of nominal** (LiFePO4 depth of discharge).

**REG tariffs** (RURA, effective 1 October 2025):

| Category | RWF/kWh |
|---|---|
| Residential 0–20 kWh/month | 89 |
| Residential 20–50 kWh/month | 310 |
| Residential above 50 kWh/month | 369 |
| Commercial below 100 kWh/month | 355 |
| **Commercial above 100 kWh/month** | **376** |
| Schools and health facilities | 214 |
| Hotels under 660,000 kWh/year | 239 |
| Industrial (small / medium / large / XL) | 175 / 133 / 110 / 97 |

Tariffs are reviewed every four months.

**Decided: all public-facing copy and calculators use RWF 400/kWh** as the business rate.
That is what actually lands on a client's bill once charges are added, and it is the figure
every SolarHope calculation should be based on. The table above is reference only — do not
print 376 as "the rate" anywhere on the site, or the pages will contradict each other. If a
page needs to explain the gap, the line is that 376 is the energy rate and 400 is what the
bill comes to once everything is added.

**Standard warranty terms:** panels 10 years · inverter 5 years · battery 5 years ·
installation workmanship 2 years.

**Payment terms:** the full project cost is payable before works commence. SolarHope does
not offer instalments or financing. Split percentages are granted on request only, never
advertised as a default.

## 5. Brand

**Design direction: minimalistic (decided 21 Nov 2026).** Generous white space, one accent
colour (gold/yellow), thin lines instead of heavy cards and shadows, and few elements per
section. Motion is quiet: a gentle fade-in on scroll and one purposeful interaction per
page at most. No decorative clutter (floating shapes, patterns, pulsing badges, tilted
photos, glows).

**The existing site design stays.** This work is an extension, not a redesign. Every new
page and section must be built from the visual language already on the site and in the
company's marketing material — same colours, same type treatment, same component shapes.
Do not introduce a new design system, swap the typeface, or restyle existing pages.
If a new section needs a pattern that does not exist yet, derive it from what is already
there.

## 6. Tone and content rules

- **Write like a business, not an engineer (decided 21 Nov 2026).** Web pages are sales
  copy: lead with what the customer gains (staying open, lower costs, no generator
  fuel or noise), keep sentences short and confident. No calculations, kWh, kW or
  tariff figures in the main copy; if working must be shown, put it in a collapsed
  "how we estimate this" note. Do not name REG or quote the price of grid power;
  say "your electricity bill" or "the grid". The honesty rules below still apply.
- **Plain language over jargon.** Say "runs two fridges and a freezer", not "4 kW
  continuous load". Avoid watts, HP and kWp in customer-facing body copy — they belong in
  spec tables only.
- **Never overclaim backup.** Never print "24 hours of backup" or similar. Backup is
  always stated as hours **at a named load** ("about 6 hours with the full bar running").
  This is a hard rule — an inflated backup figure is the fastest way to lose a client.
- **Savings are what the client stops buying from REG**, calculated from generation. Never
  imply a bill goes to zero; how much of the evening is covered depends on battery size.
- **Quote honesty is a differentiator.** SolarHope's position is that a real quote shows
  panels × watts (not a single kWp figure), usable battery capacity (not nominal), and
  backup hours at the client's actual load. Lean on this.
- Prices shown publicly must say **excluding VAT**.
- Second person ("your bill", "your roof") is correct for web and social copy. Formal
  documents — proposals, contracts, reports — use a third-person professional register
  instead.

## 7. Positioning — the thing to fix

The current site is built on an unmodified charity template. It has **Donate**,
**Volunteer**, **Sponsor System** and **Support Us** in the navigation, a "Join the
SolarHope Movement" call to action, and describes the company as "a social-impact company".

**This is wrong and it must come out.** SolarHope sells systems to paying customers. A
procurement officer or a business owner landing on a Donate button will not convert.
If the company is not a registered non-profit, soliciting donations is also a legal
exposure, not just a positioning problem.

The company *does* pursue donor- and grant-funded institutional projects. That audience is
served by a clearly separated **Partners & Funders** section — not by blending charity
language into the commercial homepage.

## 8. Never publish

- **The equipment supplier's name.** SolarHope sources hardware through a distributor whose
  name must never appear in any client-facing or public material. If you find it in the
  codebase, remove it. *(Clarified 21 Nov 2026: this is the distributor only. The
  equipment **manufacturer's** logo on inverters and batteries in photos and videos is
  fine to show.)*
- **Client personal data** — national ID or passport numbers, scanned signatures, contract
  pages. These exist in company documents; none of it goes on the website.
- **Client names in case studies without written permission.** Get sign-off per client
  before naming them; otherwise describe them generically ("a boarding school in Ruhango").
- Savings, payback or backup figures that have not been checked against §4.
